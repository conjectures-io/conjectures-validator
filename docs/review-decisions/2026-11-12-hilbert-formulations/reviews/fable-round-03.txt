## 1. Remaining defects

**Mathematical: none found.** The scrutiny you asked for is below; each item was checked by hand.

*Class field theory (`Hilbert12ClassField`):*
- **Ray class group.** `divisorsAway`, `IsPrincipalDivisor`, `CongruentOne` and `principalRay` give the fractional ideals coprime to the modulus, modulo principal ideals generated by x ≡ 1 at the finite part and positive at the listed real places. The sign of the exponent in `IsPrincipalDivisor` is right. The subgroup closure is harmless because the generating set is already a subgroup.
- **`IsClassField`.** It says exactly that the modulus is admissible for E and that the Artin map has kernel H:
  - `FrobeniusAt` forces every prime outside the modulus to be unramified and pins the arithmetic Frobenius.
  - `UnramifiedAtRealPlaces` handles the infinite part.
  - The Artin map is determined, because prime classes generate the group.
- **`ClassFieldClassification`.** All four clauses are true statements of the standard theory: finiteness, existence and uniqueness for every subgroup, every abelian extension arising, and order reversal. Uniqueness holds because the kernel fixes the set of split primes.
- **`IsConductor`.** A least admissible modulus exists and is itself admissible, since E is a class field for n exactly when E lies in the ray class field of n. Uniqueness follows from antisymmetry of `Divides`.
- **`ConductorDiscriminant`.** This is the conductor–discriminant product formula with character conductors taken as conductors of kernel-fixed fields. The trivial character gives the base field, the trivial modulus and the unit ideal, so nothing degenerates. Requiring the finset to contain every character blocks the empty-product loophole.
- **`CyclotomicReciprocity`.** True as stated, including the edge cases n = 1 and a = 0.
- **`RationalAllArguments`.** This is Kronecker–Weber with all rational arguments.

*Elasticity bridge (`Hilbert6Rigid`):*
- **`ElasticityToStiff` is a true statement.** For a C² solution:
  - The stress is C¹ in x, so the divergence theorem applies on the box.
  - Zero traction on the faces kills the boundary terms. This gives conservation of linear momentum and of energy.
  - Differentiating frame indifference along skew directions gives symmetry of P·Fᵀ, hence conservation of angular momentum.
  - Coercivity with constant c then gives the `StiffContinuum` bound with constant max(2, 1/c)·C.
- **`PolynomialMaterialWitness` is true.** For det F > 0, write F = RU with U positive; then |U² − I| ≥ |U − I| gives W ≥ dist². For det F ≤ 0, the determinant term gives W ≥ 1, and a crude bound gives dist² ≤ 24·W. So c = 1/24 works globally.
- **Indices.** The stress and divergence index conventions are consistent with `gradient`.
- **Conditional status.** The composite with the rigid limit is correctly labelled as depending on smooth solutions on a common interval.

*Other additions:*
- `EulerLocalExistence` and `NSFLocalExistence` are true statements. The global-in-time smoothness convention is satisfiable by smooth extension.
- The concentration clause in `KineticConvergence` is the right one.
- `HarnackSharpBound` is correct, including natural-number subtraction at d = 1 and d = 2.
- `QuarticSurfaceMaximum` is the known value.
