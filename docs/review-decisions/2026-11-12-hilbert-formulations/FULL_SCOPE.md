# Source scope and interpretation

The authority is Hilbert's 1900 text in the authorized 1902 English translation,
checked against the AMS reprint and specialist sources. The user asked for
formalizations, not proofs of the conjectures or computation of every requested
classification. Fable's second review explicitly agrees with that distinction.

For **6**, the chosen interpretation covers probability axioms and means,
classical mechanics, particle-to-kinetic/fluid limits, continuum-to-rigid limits,
compatibility by actual model witnesses, and equivalence of mechanical
presentations. The local elasticity bridge supplies continuum dynamics; global
balances are not silently substituted for a material equation. The gas variants
and their source deviations are declared. Phrases about all possible physical
theories and a survey of all consequences describe a research programme; they
do not require an invented exhaustive physics ontology or first-order encoding.

For **12**, arithmetic classification is an output task equipped with explicit
ray-class reciprocity, existence, and conductor–discriminant statements. The
rational and corrected imaginary-quadratic constructions are specified. A
proposed general analytic construction is assessed through a free, field-attached
recipe contract. This contract is not a sufficient test of explicitness: a
checked identity-reencoding lemma demonstrates the unrestricted interface's
weakness. No universal impossibility theorem for stronger admissibility rules
is claimed. Rational-point conditions are an optional benchmark profile, and
reciprocity actions are specific to named constructions. The Stark statement
is a separate concrete route, not identified with the entire twelfth problem.

For **algebraic 13**, the exact assertion RD(7)=3 is accepted by Fable. It is the
modern algebraic version requested by the user; the continuous-function version
is a different historical question. The upper bound is known; no proof of the
open lower bound is claimed.

For **16**, the classification request is represented by an output type with
actual catalogues and proof obligations. Its primary algebraic class is
nonsingular projective curves and surfaces. The cycle tasks specify both affine
and projective families, with exact counts and ambient isotopy arrangements.
Computability is an optional stronger request. Existence of a smooth-plane-curve
catalogue in principle is not described as the remaining open classification
content. No actual classification output has been constructed in this packet.

Fable's fourth review confirms acceptance of revision 10 after all wording
conditions were applied. The author agrees with that scoped verdict. Every source
clause has an explicit treatment under the declared interpretation. The metadata
records `clause_complete_under_declared_interpretation: true` separately from
`full_request_complete: false`. The latter remains false because #6 uses selected
physical models and #12 does not certify explicitness of an arbitrary general
construction. Neither compilation nor broad topic coverage closes that gap.
No theorem says that future sharper interpretations or admissibility rules are
impossible; both the author and Fable explicitly reject that stronger claim.
