## What the informal problem asks

Erdős Problem 939 asks: if `r ≥ 4`, can the sum of `r − 2` coprime `r`-powerful numbers ever be
itself `r`-powerful? A positive integer is `r`-powerful when every prime dividing it does so to at
least the `r`-th power. The question is about genuine `r`-powerful numbers — the source's own
recorded examples are Cambie's, for `r = 5`, `r = 7`, and `r = 8`. No example is recorded for
`r = 4`.

## What the published Lean task asks

At the pinned source revision, `FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/939.lean` defines:

```lean
def Erdos939Sums (r : ℕ) :=
    {S : Finset ℕ | S.card = r - 2 ∧ S.Coprime ∧ r.Full (∑ s ∈ S, s) ∧ ∀ s ∈ S, r.Full s}
```

and the target is `answer(sorry) ↔ ∀ r ≥ 4, (Erdos939Sums r).Nonempty`.

`Nat.Full r n` holds when every prime divisor `p` of `n` satisfies `p ^ r ∣ n`. The condition is
vacuous for `n = 1`, which has no prime divisors, and for `n = 0`, which every prime power divides.
The pinned source itself carries `Nat.Full.one_right` and `Nat.Full.zero_right` as lemmas.
`Erdos939Sums` imposes no lower bound on the elements of `S`.

## What the submitted proof does

The submission splits the target into three legs (`main_claim`).

**`r ≥ 6` — a genuine construction.** Roughly 670 lines build, for each `r ≥ 6`, infinitely many
identities of the required shape. The method starts from the binomial identity

```
(X + Y)^r = (X − Y)^r + 2 Σ_{j odd} C(r,j) X^{r−j} Y^j
```

with `X = m^r` for a large prime `m` and `Y = B^r` where `B` is chosen divisible by every
coefficient `2·C(r,j)` and by each of the split coefficients. Each summand is then `r`-powerful:
the prime factors of the coefficient are absorbed by `B^{rj}`, and those of `X` appear to exponent
`r(r−j)`. The `j = 3` term is split into `r/2 − 2` distinct positive multiples so that the total
number of summands lands exactly on `r − 2`. Distinctness is proved through the `m`-adic
valuation, and joint coprimality holds because `(X − Y)^r` is coprime to `Y` while every other
summand is divisible by `B`. Infinitude follows from choosing arbitrarily large primes `m`.

**`r = 5` — Cambie's example.** `leg_five` exhibits `{2⁸·3¹⁰·5⁷, 2¹²·23⁶, 11⁵·13⁵}`, which is the
example quoted verbatim in the docstring of the sibling declaration
`Erdos939.erdos_939.variants.examples` in the pinned source.

**`r = 4` — the degenerate leg.** This is the decisive part:

```lean
/-- The `r = 4` leg: `{0, 1}` is a valid member since `0` and `1` are `r`-full. -/
theorem leg_four : (Erdos939.Erdos939Sums 4).Nonempty := by
  refine ⟨{0, 1}, by decide, by decide, ?_, ?_⟩
  · simpa using Nat.Full.one_right 4
  · intro s hs
    fin_cases hs
    · exact Nat.Full.zero_right 4
    · exact Nat.Full.one_right 4
```

`{0, 1}` satisfies every published conjunct: its cardinality is `2 = 4 − 2`; its gcd is `1`; its sum
is `1`, which is `4`-full; and both elements are `4`-full. It is not a sum of `r − 2` coprime
`r`-powerful numbers in any sense the informal problem intends.

## Why this is a formalization defect

The published target is a universally quantified statement over all `r ≥ 4`. It is established only
because the `r = 4` instance admits a degenerate witness that the informal problem excludes.
Removing that witness leaves `r = 4` unproven and the target unproven with it. The defect is an
omitted hypothesis: `Erdos939Sums` should require its summands to be positive, and on the natural
reading of the informal problem greater than `1`.

One point examined and cleared: `Finset.Coprime` here is joint coprimality (`S.gcd id = 1`), not
pairwise. That initially reads as a second weakening, but the source's own `r = 5` example has its
first two elements sharing a factor of `2`, so joint coprimality is the intended notion. This is not
a defect.

The miner did not weaken, replace, or alter the challenge — the target is bound through
`fcTypeOfName%` and the Comparator confirmed statement equality. The mismatch was present in the
task we published, so `TRIVIALISED_STATEMENT` does not apply.

## Note on the `r ≥ 6` construction

The public rationale should state plainly that the bulk of this submission is not an exploit. The
`r ≥ 6` construction is a substantial piece of formalized mathematics that establishes infinitely
many valid identities for every `r ≥ 6`, and it would settle the corresponding portion of the
informal problem on its own terms. It does not settle the published target, because that target is
a statement about all `r ≥ 4` and the `r = 4` case is the one the construction does not reach.

The submitted file describes itself as a formalization of a document named `E939_Partial.tex`. No
evidence of prior publication or misattribution was found or sought beyond the record, and under
policy `v1` an open-ended search to create doubt after a valid result arrives is not conducted.
`MISATTRIBUTED_WORK` does not apply.

If the team corrects the source and reissues the target with a positivity hypothesis, the miner's
`r ≥ 6` and `r = 5` legs would transfer unchanged; only `r = 4` would remain open. That is worth
saying in the public rationale so the miner understands what would qualify.
